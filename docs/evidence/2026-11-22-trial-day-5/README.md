## What it proves

- **"Assignment required" works.** An account outside the four role groups is refused by Entra itself, before the site is involved.
- **SharePoint follows the groups.** A barista reads what the website publishes, can't change it, and can't see or open the managers' Schedule.
- **The Conditional Access policies are aimed at the right thing.** They match Ethan on the staff portal and don't touch his SharePoint sign-in. The MFA policy would have challenged him (he signed in without MFA), so switching it On will be felt; and the 8-hour session control would apply.
- **SharePoint changes are recorded by account and time,** and the record was enough to explain a real mistake (below).

## The 2026-11-07 finding: closed

On 2026-11-07 Michael's own account, which holds no staff role, got a token for `bc-website-staff` despite "Assignment required", and only the site's role check refused it. That account is a **Global Administrator**, and Microsoft's page says: "Users with a Global Administrator role can sign in to applications, regardless of the assignment required settings" (Microsoft Learn, "Properties of an enterprise application", dated 2026-10-20, checked 2026-11-23). Riley, who isn't an administrator, was refused by Entra (`01`, `02`). So it's Microsoft's design, not a gap in the set-up, and the site's own check is the second line for Global Administrators (`03` and `03b`: let in by Entra, refused by the site). The site's role check stays.
